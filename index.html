Accessibility HCI case study

AccessGuide AI

A prototype for helping older adults and low-digital-literacy users complete unfamiliar phone tasks with calmer explanations, smaller steps, and built-in help.

Problem

Support exists, but people often cannot find it, understand it, or ask for help with confidence.

Design idea

Turn one confusing task into a guided interaction with plain language and reversible actions.

What to evaluate

Whether the interface reduces hesitation, confusion, and help-seeking friction.

Target users

Older adults who know what they want to do, but not always where to find it.

The design assumes users may feel uncertain, rushed, or embarrassed when a familiar task becomes technical.

Design principles

  • One next step at a time
  • Plain language before jargon
  • Reversible actions
  • Help-seeking without shame

How to evaluate

  • Task completion
  • Self-reported confidence
  • Help-seeking frequency
  • Perceived cognitive load

Interaction design

How the prototype works

Choose a task, change the explanation pace, and see how the support adapts for different levels of confidence.

Slower pace = more guidance

    Why it matters

    Older adults should not need to guess how to ask for help.

    The interface is designed around task friction, confidence, and autonomy rather than visual polish alone.

    Research notes

    Good fit for user studies

    This prototype can be used to test whether clearer structure, simpler language, and reversible steps improve comfort and completion.

    Design goal

    Reduce confusion, protect confidence, and make help-seeking feel normal.
